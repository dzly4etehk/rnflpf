Why do antihistamine sleep aids often cause morning grogginess?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Antihistamine sleep aids often cause morning grogginess, also known as a "hangover effect." This happens because their sedative properties linger in the body well beyond bedtime, continuing to affect alertness, focus, and coordination the next day.

Explanation

Antihistamine sleep aids can make you feel sleepy the next morning because they have effects that last beyond the time you take them. This is known as a 'residual sedative effect.' According to research, these medications can affect how alert you feel, even after you wake up.

How Antihistamines Work

Sedative Effects: Antihistamines are designed to help with allergies, but they also make you sleepy. This is because they block a chemical in your brain called histamine, which helps keep you awake.

Lingering Effects: Some antihistamines stay in your system for a long time. This means they can keep making you feel sleepy even after you wake up.

What Studies Show

One study found that the sedative effects can last into the next day, making people groggy in the morning.

Another study showed that people might get used to these sleepy effects over time, but not everyone does.
