# What Happened to My Body After Testing a Vibration Plate for 2 Months

What Happened to My Body After Testing a Vibration Plate for 2 Months

I’ve dealt with aching legs, chronic bruising, and cellulite since puberty. I always thought it was just me and an iron deficiency. But recently, after a fair amount of research and a couple of consultations, I learned it’s likely lipedema.

[1]

I’m not usually one to jump on viral health trends. As a health editor who’s tested seemingly endless products, I’m skeptical of anything that’s overly hyped on social media or marketed as a panacea for several conditions. Vibration plates, however, are a different animal.

[2]

What Do Vibration Plates Do?

Vibration plates do exactly what the name implies: They shake side to side or up and down, creating rapid vibrations that challenge your muscles to stabilize your body. This is meant to create enough instability that your muscles must constantly adapt, challenging your balance and stability.

[3]

[4]

Considering how aggressively these plates can shake, they’re not ideal for anyone who’s pregnant, has a pacemaker, has recently undergone surgery, has a severe balance disorder, or is at high risk of fracture. Since I don’t live with any of these conditions, I felt safe proceeding with my test, although I was still quite dubious that it would deliver on any of the above claims.

My Vibration Plate Experience

The first time I used my Sunny Fitness Fitboard 2D Vibration Platform, it was genuinely fun. The little shakes tickled my skin at first (increased circulation can trigger a slight histamine reaction), but I quickly got used to them and tried some squats and calf raises.

It feels more challenging when you move your feet as far apart as possible on the platform (influencers claim it’s as physically exerting as running, but I definitely didn’t find that). I highly recommend speaking to your partner or friend in a funny voice while on a vibration plate; it gives my husband and me endless amusement.

[to My Body](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/6766313097.html)

The plate has 99 speed levels and five built-in workout programs, plus a manual mode that lets you control the intensity yourself. Programs are automatically set to 10 minutes, but you can increase the time (though the company doesn’t recommend staying on the platform for more than 30 minutes per session). I’ve found 10 minutes is more than enough for my needs.

[Body After Testing Vibration](https://repair-cascade.jcesqc8.com/blog/4803801699.html)

You control the device with a small remote with a wristband, so you can keep it on you if you want. The platform has a grippy rubber surface, which helps me feel secure whether I’m wearing sneakers or not. It’s surprisingly quiet, even on one of the highest speed settings, but you need to make sure it’s on flat, stable ground, or it will start to rumble.

After the first month of using the vibration plate at least every other day, my legs felt stronger and lighter. I never just stand on the platform, though; I do squats, lunges, bridge lifts, and balances. Some unexpected benefits I’ve noticed: better digestion and relief of muscle tension. I also feel more energized after a session. I’ve tried sitting on it, but that wasn’t the most comfortable for me. If I haven’t drunk a ton of water that day, it can leave me a touch dizzy, but that subsides once I sit down.

[My Body After Testing](https://tendonforge.dns0663.com/)

Two months down, and I now consider myself a vibration plate convert. I love how I feel both on and off the platform: lighter, more energized, and stronger. My skin also feels tighter on my legs, arms, and face. I’ve even begun to notice modest improvements in my cellulite, something I really didn’t expect. While I can’t say whether the vibration plate deserves all the credit, it’s the first time I’ve personally noticed a visible improvement here.

I plan to keep my test going for the next six months to see whether I notice any further strength gains and improvements in my skin’s appearance. But so far, I’m thrilled with the progress and relieved there’s something I can consistently do that seems to help a condition that’s notoriously difficult to manage.
