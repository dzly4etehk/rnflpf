Internal Medicine

Severe rib pain is most commonly caused by three conditions: a broken (fractured) rib, costochondritis (inflammation of the cartilage connecting the ribs to the breastbone), or intercostal neuralgia (irritation or damage to the nerves running between the ribs). Each cause presents differently — fractures typically follow trauma, costochondritis produces tenderness at the rib-sternum joint, and neuralgia often causes sharp, burning, or shooting pain along the nerve path.

Explanation

There are several reasons why you might experience severe pain in your ribs. One possibility is a rib fracture. This is when one or more rib bones break, usually from a strong impact or injury, and it can cause a very sharp pain that worsens with breathing or movement.

Another common cause of severe rib pain is costochondritis. This happens when the cartilage that connects the ribs to the breastbone (sternum) becomes inflamed. The area can feel very tender, and even a light touch might hurt. A third possible cause is intercostal neuralgia. This condition occurs when the nerves running between the ribs are irritated or compressed, leading to a burning, stabbing, or aching pain along the rib cage. Each of these causes produces pain in different ways, so a doctor might ask about how the pain started, if there was an injury, or if it gets worse with certain movements.
