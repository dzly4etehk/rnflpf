Gut on Fire? Why Your Colon Is Inflamed and Your Medical Path to Relief

If your gut feels like it's "on fire," you may be dealing with colitis—a medical term for inflammation of the colon (large intestine). Colitis is not a single disease. It's a condition with several possible causes, ranging from infections to autoimmune disorders like ulcerative colitis.

Colon inflammation can be painful and disruptive. But with the right diagnosis and treatment plan, most people can control symptoms and protect their long-term health.

Let's break down what colitis is, why it happens, and what you can do next.

What Is Colitis?

Colitis simply means inflammation of the colon. When the colon becomes inflamed, it can't absorb water properly or move stool normally. This leads to symptoms such as:

Persistent diarrhea (sometimes with blood or mucus)

Abdominal pain or cramping

Urgency to have a bowel movement

Fatigue

Bloating

Unintended weight loss

Fever (in some cases)

Symptoms can range from mild to severe. Some people experience flare-ups followed by periods of remission. Others may have ongoing symptoms that require continuous treatment.

2. Infectious Colitis

This type of colitis is caused by bacteria, viruses, or parasites. Common triggers include:

Contaminated food or water

Recent travel

Antibiotic use (which can disrupt healthy gut bacteria)

Infectious colitis often develops suddenly and may include:

Fever

Severe diarrhea

Dehydration

Many cases improve with proper treatment, though some infections can be serious if left untreated.

3. Ischemic Colitis

Ischemic colitis happens when blood flow to the colon is reduced.

Risk factors include:

Older age

Heart disease

Blood clotting disorders

Low blood pressure episodes

Symptoms often appear suddenly and may include:

Sharp abdominal pain

Bloody stool

This condition can be mild or life-threatening, depending on severity. Immediate medical evaluation is important if symptoms are sudden and severe.

4. Microscopic Colitis

Microscopic colitis causes chronic watery diarrhea but may not show visible inflammation during a colonoscopy. It is diagnosed through biopsy.

Why Colon Inflammation Shouldn't Be Ignored

Mild digestive upset happens to everyone. But ongoing colon inflammation is different.

Untreated colitis can lead to complications such as:

Severe dehydration

Anemia from blood loss

Malnutrition

Colon perforation (rare but serious)

Increased colon cancer risk (in long-standing ulcerative colitis)

This is not meant to alarm you—but to emphasize the importance of proper diagnosis and care.

If you have persistent diarrhea, blood in your stool, or severe abdominal pain, speak to a doctor promptly.

How Doctors Diagnose Colitis

Your healthcare provider will start with a medical history and physical exam. From there, testing may include:

Blood tests (to check for infection, inflammation, anemia)

Stool tests (to rule out infection)

Colonoscopy (to view the colon and take biopsies)

Imaging tests like CT scans (in some cases)

Accurate diagnosis is critical because treatments differ depending on the type of colitis.

Lifestyle Steps That Support Healing

Medical treatment is essential, but daily habits matter too.

Here are practical steps that may help manage colitis symptoms:

Stay hydrated, especially during diarrhea episodes

Eat smaller, more frequent meals

Limit high-fat, spicy, or heavily processed foods during flares

Keep a food diary to identify triggers

Manage stress through exercise, therapy, or relaxation techniques

Avoid smoking (smoking worsens many forms of colitis)

Diet is highly individual. Some people tolerate fiber well, others do not. A registered dietitian familiar with inflammatory bowel disease can help personalize your plan.

When to Seek Urgent Care

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

Severe abdominal pain

Heavy rectal bleeding

Signs of dehydration (dizziness, fainting, very little urine)

High fever with diarrhea

Rapid heart rate

These can signal serious complications that require prompt care.

Living Well With Colitis

A diagnosis of colitis—especially ulcerative colitis—can feel overwhelming at first. But many people live full, active lives with proper treatment and monitoring.

Key long-term strategies include:
