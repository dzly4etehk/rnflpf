Vitamin A is essential for cellular growth, including hair which is the fastest growing tissue in the body. This vitamin includes compounds such as retinol, retinoic acid, and retinal. Another important factor that Vitamin A brings is that it produces sebum, which keeps the scalp oily and contributes to superior hair quality and keeps it healthy.

People who are experiencing Vitamin A deficiency experience hair loss. Vitamin A is found in pumpkin, spinach, kale, and carrots.

As with everything, too much of one thing can be detrimental, and this holds true for Vitamin A because over supplementing can lead to hair loss. Because of this, finding the right balance of the amount of Vitamin A you are taking is important.

B-complex vitamins are essential for metabolism and can tremendously help hair growth. Biotin or Vitamin B7 is especially important and is considered the most popular vitamin that can aid hair growth. Biotin can help strengthen hair and improve its overall condition. It also helps with keratin production, which is a major component of hair. Biotin’s most common food sources include bananas, salmon, sweet potato, and milk.

People who are pregnant or breastfeeding can suffer from lower levels of Biotin, as well as individuals who are on epilepsy medicine. Having low levels of Biotin can lead to brittle nails and hair loss.

B Vitamins help with hair growth by helping create red blood cells, which carry nutrients and oxygen to the scalp and follicles. A popular source of B Vitamins in food includes meat, seafood, almonds, and whole grains.

Ascorbic Acid or Vitamin C can boost your immune system by fighting free radicals that can also block hair growth. This vitamin also creates collagen, which is an important part of the hair structure. It can also help absorb iron which is another vital mineral that contributes to proper hair growth. The common food sources of Vitamin C are citrus fruits.

Another important role that Vitamin C plays in hair growth is effective blood circulation. Vitamin C helps improve blood circulation all over your body, including your scalp, which stimulates the hair follicles and is instrumental in hair growth.

Alopecia is an autoimmune disorder that leads to hair loss and, in severe cases, at a rapid rate. In some cases, Vitamin D deficiency is linked to Alopecia because it plays an important role in hair production.

Zinc plays an important role in making the protein in hair and cells. Supplementing with Zinc is needed because your body does not produce this mineral naturally. People who suffer from low levels of Zinc suffer from hair loss and a weakened sense of smell or taste.

Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding tend to have lower levels of Zinc, as well as people who suffer from kidney diseases.

Iron is a micronutrient that helps with blood and oxygen circulation, especially in cells. Iron-deficient individuals can experience a slowdown in the production of hemoglobin which affects the oxygen delivery to the scalp area and hinders hair growth. Other symptoms of iron deficiency include hair loss and fatigue.

Iron deficiency is common and occurs in women who experience heavy periods as well as people who are suffering from chronic diseases.
