Does finasteride affect testosterone or make me less ‘manly’?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Finasteride does influence hormone activity, but it does not lower overall testosterone or make you less "manly." The medication works by blocking the enzyme 5-alpha reductase, which converts testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). As a result, DHT levels drop while total testosterone typically stays the same or rises slightly. Because DHT—not testosterone—is the main driver of male-pattern hair loss and prostate enlargement, finasteride is effective for these conditions without reducing masculinity. Some men do report side effects such as changes in libido or mood, but these are not universal and often resolve after stopping the medication.

Explanation

Finasteride is a medication that can influence testosterone levels in the body. According to the studies provided, finasteride works by blocking the conversion of testosterone into another hormone called dihydrotestosterone (DHT). This can lead to a slight increase in testosterone levels in the bloodstream.

Testosterone Effects: Finasteride does not significantly lower testosterone levels; instead, it may cause a slight increase. This is because it stops testosterone from turning into DHT, which is linked to hair loss and prostate growth.

Masculinity Concerns: There is no evidence from the provided studies that finasteride makes someone less 'manly'. Masculinity involves many factors, including hormones, but also personal identity and social roles.

In summary, while finasteride can change hormone levels slightly, it does not make someone less masculine.
