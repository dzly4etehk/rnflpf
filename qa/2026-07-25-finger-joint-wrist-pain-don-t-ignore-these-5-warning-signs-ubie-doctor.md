What might cause pain in both my finger joints and wrist?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Pain in your finger joints and wrist is often caused by rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, or other conditions affecting the hand, such as joint degeneration, tendonitis, or carpal tunnel syndrome. Identifying the underlying cause early is key to preventing long-term damage and finding effective relief.

Explanation

Pain in both your finger joints and wrist can have several causes. One common cause is rheumatoid arthritis, which can lead to joint pain, swelling, and stiffness. This condition often affects the small joints in the hands, including the fingers and wrist. Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic inflammatory disorder that can cause the immune system to mistakenly attack the body's own tissues.

Another possible cause is degeneration of the joints, which can occur due to wear and tear over time. This can lead to conditions like osteoarthritis, where the cartilage that cushions the joints wears down, causing pain and swelling.
