What to Consider

Length

When it comes to length, inseam makes all the difference. Below, we break down the most common lengths.

5-inch

A 5-inch short sits high on the thigh and gives off a fast, sporty vibe. It’s the go-to for runners or anyone who wants minimal fabric for maximum mobility, but it can also feel a little revealing.

7-inch

A 7-inch inseam hits mid-thigh, giving you enough coverage for squats and lunges while still looking athletic. It’s versatile enough for the gym, a run, or casual wear.

9-inch

On the other hand, a 9-inch inseam drapes closer to the knee. It’s more modest, relaxed, and even a bit retro, which makes it popular for lifting sessions, basketball, or just hanging out. The trade-off is that it can feel bulkier and restrict lunges and stretches.

Material

Today, the standard for a good pair of shorts starts with premium features like 4-way stretch, water-resistance, odor resistance and moisture-wicking capabilities. Quality athletic shorts are lightweight and breathable too—with many brands going the extra mile to create their own synthetic fabrics or add in their unique liners. When it comes to fabrics, these are the best ones to look out for.

Polyester

Polyester is lightweight, durable, and commonly blended with spandex or elastane for stretch. It wicks sweat and dries quickly, keeping you cool and comfortable during even the sweatiest workouts. Because of its durability, it holds up well after repeated washes, making it ideal for running, training, or any multi-use athletic shorts that need to move with your body.

Nylon

Nylon feels smooth, soft, and breathable against the skin, often with a slightly “swishy” texture that makes it comfortable to wear. It dries quickly and resists wind, which makes it practical for outdoor activities. Nylon shorts are especially versatile, performing well for cross-training, hiking, or even casual wear where lightweight, durable fabric is key.

Liner

Many of the newer athletic shorts from the best men's clothing brands come with built-in briefs or compression liners. They add support, reduce chafing, and eliminate the need for extra layers, which is useful during long runs. But liners can also feel restrictive and take longer to dry. Shorts without liners are lighter and more breathable, but you’ll probably want to pair them with compression underwear if you’re training hard.

Pockets

Pockets are another key detail. For running or training, zippered pockets keep keys or a phone from bouncing around, while drop-in side pockets are fine for quick storage in the gym. If you plan to wear your shorts casually, deeper side pockets are a must. Placement matters too—low or bulky pockets can flap around and get in the way of movement.
