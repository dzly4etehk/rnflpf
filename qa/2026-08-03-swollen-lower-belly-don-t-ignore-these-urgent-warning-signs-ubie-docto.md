In some cases, however, lower abdominal swelling may signal an underlying medical condition such as an ovarian cyst, uterine fibroids, urinary retention, or, less commonly, an infection or growth that requires medical evaluation.

Explanation

When your lower belly feels swollen, one common reason is abdominal bloating. This happens when too much gas builds up in your stomach and intestines, which can be influenced by certain foods, eating too quickly, food intolerances, or other digestive issues. Sometimes, constipation or changes in your gut can also lead to a feeling of fullness and swelling. Fluid retention is another cause—as your body holds onto extra water, your belly may appear distended. In more serious cases, a condition called ascites could cause swelling if there is an abnormal buildup of fluid in the abdomen, but this is less common. Hormonal changes, stress, or even certain gastrointestinal disorders such as irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) may contribute to increased abdominal distension as well. In women, a swollen abdomen may also be due to gynecological problems and should be evaluated. Because there are many possible causes, if your lower belly swelling is new, becomes uncomfortable, or is accompanied by other symptoms like pain or changes in your bowel movements, it's important to get a clearer understanding of what might be causing it. You can start by taking a free symptom checker test to help identify potential causes based on your specific symptoms. A doctor can then examine you and possibly order tests to determine the exact reason and recommend the best treatment.
