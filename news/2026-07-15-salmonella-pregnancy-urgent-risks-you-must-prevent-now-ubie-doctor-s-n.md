What are the risks of salmonella infection during pregnancy and how can it be prevented?

Internal Medicine

Salmonella infection during pregnancy is a foodborne illness caused by Salmonella bacteria, and it poses serious risks to both the pregnant person and the baby. Common sources include undercooked poultry, raw eggs, unpasteurized dairy, and contaminated produce. Symptoms typically include diarrhea, fever, abdominal cramps, nausea, and vomiting, usually appearing 6 hours to 6 days after exposure.

During pregnancy, salmonella can lead to dehydration, bacteremia (bacteria in the bloodstream), preterm labor, miscarriage, or transmission to the newborn. Prevention is key: cook meats thoroughly, avoid raw eggs and unpasteurized products, wash produce carefully, and practice strict hand hygiene.
