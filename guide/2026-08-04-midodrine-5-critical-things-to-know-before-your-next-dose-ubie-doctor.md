Midodrine: 5 Important Things Doctors Want You to Know

Midodrine is a prescription medication that increases blood pressure by tightening blood vessels. It's often used for people who feel dizzy or faint when standing (orthostatic hypotension) and, in specialized settings, to support circulation and kidney function in advanced liver disease. Here are five key points your doctor wants you to understand about midodrine.

1. What Midodrine Is and How It Works

Midodrine is a "prodrug" that converts in the body to desglymidodrine, an alpha-1 agonist.

By stimulating alpha-1 receptors on blood vessels, it causes vessels to narrow, raising blood pressure and improving blood flow to vital organs.

Orthostatic hypotension (low blood pressure upon standing)

Cirrhosis with refractory ascites and hepatorenal syndrome (when combined with albumin and octreotide)

In cirrhotic patients with low arterial pressure and poor kidney function, midodrine has been shown to improve mean arterial pressure and glomerular filtration rate (Planas et al., 2001). The European Association for the Study of the Liver (2014) includes midodrine-based regimens as an option when terlipressin is unavailable.

2. Who May Benefit Most

Doctors prescribe midodrine for two main groups:

Orthostatic hypotension

You feel lightheaded, dizzy, or faint upon standing.

Causes include autonomic failure, diabetic neuropathy, or medication side effects.

Advanced liver disease with renal dysfunction

Increase arterial pressure

Reduce activation of hormones that worsen kidney function

Improve urine output and electrolytes (Planas et al., 2001)

Studies show that patients with low sodium levels (hyponatremia) and cirrhosis face higher mortality (Kim et al., 2008). By supporting circulation, midodrine may help stabilize kidney function and sodium balance.

4. How Midodrine Is Dosed and Monitored

Proper dosing and monitoring help maximize benefits and minimize risks:

Starting dose and schedule:

Typical start: 2.5 mg three times a day (morning, midday and late afternoon)

Maximum: 10 mg three times daily, adjusted by your doctor

Monitoring tips:

Check blood pressure while lying down (supine) and standing before treatment and periodically thereafter.

Avoid a dose within 4 hours of bedtime to minimize nighttime hypertension.

Regular blood tests to monitor kidney function and electrolytes, especially in liver disease.

Report any symptoms of high blood pressure (headache, blurred vision) or urinary issues to your healthcare provider.
