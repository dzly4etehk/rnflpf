# The Keto Warning: Why Staying in Ketosis Too Long Might Hurt Your Hormonal Health | Ubie Doctor's Note

The Keto Warning: Why Staying in Ketosis Too Long Might Hurt Your Hormonal Health

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Prolonged ketosis can disrupt hormonal health by raising cortisol, lowering active thyroid hormone (T3), and altering sex hormones. Over time, this reduces metabolic flexibility and may cause fatigue, hair thinning, mood changes, irregular periods, and reduced carb tolerance.

Who's most at risk? Women, athletes, people with thyroid conditions, and those under chronic stress. Warning signs include persistent fatigue, cold intolerance, insomnia, and menstrual irregularities. Safer strategies—like cyclical keto or targeted carbohydrate intake around workouts—can help protect hormone balance. Speak with a doctor if symptoms persist.

Explanation

The ketogenic (keto) diet has helped many people lose weight, improve blood sugar control, and feel more mentally focused—especially in the short term. But like many powerful tools, keto works best when used thoughtfully. Staying in ketosis for too long may place stress on your hormonal system, particularly if your body loses Metabolic Flexibility—the ability to switch smoothly between burning carbohydrates and fat for energy.

This article explains why long-term ketosis may affect hormonal health, who may be most at risk, and how to approach keto in a safer, more balanced way—using credible, science-based insights and plain language.

What Ketosis Does to the Body (A Quick Refresher)

Ketosis occurs when carbohydrate intake is very low, forcing the body to burn fat and produce ketones for fuel. This metabolic state can be helpful in certain situations, such as:

Short-term fat loss

Improving insulin sensitivity

Managing certain neurological conditions under medical care

However, ketosis is not how the human body evolved to function all the time. Historically, humans moved in and out of ketosis depending on food availability. That natural cycling is a key part of Metabolic Flexibility.

Why Hormones Are Sensitive to Long-Term Ketosis

Hormones are chemical messengers that rely on adequate energy, nutrients, and signaling from the brain. Extended ketosis can interfere with this balance in several ways.

1. Cortisol May Rise

Cortisol is your primary stress hormone. While helpful in short bursts, chronically elevated cortisol can disrupt other hormones.

[Doctor's Note](https://jointrevive.xianfengchem.com/blog/6604436912.html)

Research from endocrinology and metabolic health fields suggests that very low-carb diets may:

Increase cortisol output over time

Reduce the body's ability to recover from physical or emotional stress

Interfere with sleep quality

Elevated cortisol can suppress thyroid hormones and sex hormones, creating a domino effect.

2. Thyroid Hormone Conversion May Slow

The thyroid regulates metabolism, body temperature, and energy levels. Studies published in reputable metabolic journals show that prolonged carbohydrate restriction can reduce levels of T3, the active thyroid hormone.

Possible symptoms include:

Cold sensitivity

Fatigue

Hair thinning

Slower metabolism

This does not mean keto causes thyroid disease, but it may worsen symptoms in people who already have borderline thyroid function.

3. Reproductive Hormones Can Be Affected

Hormones like estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone are energy-sensitive. When the brain senses low energy availability for long periods, it may reduce reproductive hormone output.

Worsened PMS

Reduced libido

Changes in mood or sleep

Women in peri-menopause and post-menopause may be especially sensitive due to already fluctuating hormone levels. The Role of Metabolic Flexibility

Metabolic Flexibility is your body's ability to efficiently switch between fuel sources—carbohydrates when they are available, and fat when they are not.

Healthy metabolic flexibility is associated with:

Better insulin sensitivity

More stable energy levels

Healthier hormone signaling

Improved stress resilience

Long-term strict ketosis can reduce metabolic flexibility, making the body less efficient at handling carbohydrates when they are reintroduced. This may lead to:

Blood sugar swings

Rapid weight regain

Digestive discomfort

Mood changes

Ironically, the diet that once improved metabolic health may begin to limit it.

Who Should Be Especially Cautious with Long-Term Keto?

Based on guidance from endocrinology and nutrition science organizations, caution is warranted for:

Women with menstrual irregularities

Individuals with thyroid disorders

People under chronic stress or overtraining

Peri-menopausal and post-menopausal women

Anyone with a history of disordered eating

This does not mean keto is unsafe for everyone—but it does mean it is not a one-size-fits-all solution.

Can Keto Be Used More Safely?

Yes—for many people, keto works best as a phase, not a permanent state.

Evidence-informed approaches that support hormone health include:

Cyclical keto: Periodic higher-carb days

Targeted carbs: Adding carbs around workouts

Seasonal keto: Using keto for limited periods, then transitioning

Protein adequacy: Ensuring enough intake to support hormones

Stress management: Prioritizing sleep and recovery

These approaches help preserve Metabolic Flexibility while still gaining benefits from fat adaptation.

The Importance of Individualized Care

Hormonal health is deeply personal. Age, sex, genetics, stress levels, and life stage all matter.

When to Speak to a Doctor

You should speak to a doctor if you experience:

Missed periods for several months

Severe fatigue or dizziness

Heart palpitations

Significant mood changes

Unexplained weight changes

Anything that feels serious, worsening, or life-threatening should be evaluated by a medical professional promptly.

The Bottom Line

Keto can be a useful metabolic tool—but staying in ketosis too long may place unnecessary strain on your hormonal system, especially if Metabolic Flexibility is lost. Hormones thrive on balance, not extremes.

Listening to your body, allowing flexibility in your diet, and seeking medical guidance when needed can help you protect both metabolic and hormonal health—now and in the long term.
