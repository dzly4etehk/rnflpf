Is there any connection between photophobia and migraine/headache?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Photophobia (light sensitivity) is strongly linked to migraine and headache disorders. Bright light commonly triggers or worsens migraine pain, and most people with migraines report discomfort when exposed to light. This sensitivity often occurs alongside other symptoms like nausea, throbbing head pain, and sensitivity to sound, making everyday environments overwhelming during an attack.

Explanation

Photophobia means that your eyes are very sensitive to light. When you have a migraine or headache, bright lights, whether from the sun, lamps, or screens, can make you feel even worse.

During a migraine, your brain and nerves may react strongly to light, which can add to your pain and make you feel uncomfortable. This is why many people with migraines tend to seek out dark, quiet rooms when a headache starts.
