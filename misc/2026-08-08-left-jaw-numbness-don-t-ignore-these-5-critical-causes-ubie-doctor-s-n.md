Left-sided jaw numbness may result from nerve-related conditions such as acoustic neuroma (a benign tumor on the nerve connecting the ear and brain) or trigeminal neuropathies, which disrupt the trigeminal nerve responsible for facial sensation. Dental issues, injury, infections, or vascular problems can also contribute. Because jaw numbness can signal underlying nerve damage or, in rare cases, a serious event like a stroke, identifying the cause early is essential.

Numbness in the jaw can happen for several reasons, often related to nerve issues. One possible cause is an acoustic neuroma, which is a growth that can press on nearby nerves, leading to numbness or a toothache-like feeling.

Another cause could be trigeminal neuropathies, which affect the trigeminal nerve responsible for sensation in the face. This condition can start with numbness in the face, including the jaw.
