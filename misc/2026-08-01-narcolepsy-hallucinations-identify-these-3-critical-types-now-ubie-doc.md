What types of hallucinations are commonly experienced with narcolepsy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Narcolepsy hallucinations are sensory experiences—most often visual (seeing things that aren't there) or auditory (hearing sounds that aren't real)—that occur as a person falls asleep (hypnagogic) or wakes up (hypnopompic). They can feel vivid and frightening, and are caused by REM sleep intruding into wakefulness.

Because these hallucinations can mimic other neurological or psychiatric conditions, identifying the true cause matters for getting the right treatment. If you're experiencing unusual sleep-related hallucinations, excessive daytime sleepiness, or sudden muscle weakness, take a free, instant, online symptom check to better understand your symptoms and confidently plan your next steps.

People with narcolepsy can experience hallucinations, especially when they are falling asleep or waking up. These hallucinations can be visual, meaning they see things that aren't there. For example, they might see shapes, people, or even animals. Sometimes, these hallucinations can also be auditory, where they might hear sounds or voices that aren't actually happening.

Types of Hallucinations

Visual Hallucinations: These are the most common and involve seeing things that aren't real. This might include simple shapes or complex images like people or animals.

Auditory Hallucinations: These involve hearing sounds or voices that aren't present. It could be like someone talking or other noises.

These experiences can be very vivid and sometimes frightening, but they are a recognized part of narcolepsy. If anyone is struggling with these symptoms, it's important to talk to a healthcare professional.
