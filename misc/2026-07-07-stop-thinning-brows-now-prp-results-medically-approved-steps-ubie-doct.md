Why Do Brows Thin?

Eyebrow hair loss can be gradual or sudden, and it often has multiple contributing factors:

Aging: Hair grows more slowly, and follicles may shrink over time.

Hormonal changes: Menopause, thyroid problems, and other hormonal shifts can disrupt the normal hair cycle.

Nutritional deficiencies: Low iron, biotin, zinc or protein intake can starve hair follicles.

Over-plucking or waxing: Repeated trauma can damage follicles permanently.

Skin conditions: Eczema, psoriasis or dermatitis around the brow area leads to inflammation.

Autoimmune causes: Alopecia areata can target brow hairs specifically.

Medications: Chemotherapy, some blood thinners or retinoids may affect hair growth.

Understanding the underlying cause is key. If you're not sure why your brows are thinning, you may benefit from basic blood tests (thyroid, CBC, iron panel) and a skin exam by a dermatologist.

PRP for Eyebrows Before and After: What You Need to Know

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy uses your own blood's growth factors to stimulate hair follicles. It's safe, minimally invasive, and often performed in dermatology or aesthetic clinics.

How PRP Works

Blood draw: A small vial of your blood is taken.

Centrifuge: The sample is spun to separate plasma rich in platelets.

Injection: The PRP is injected into the brow area at precise depths.

Growth stimulation: Platelets release growth factors that may jump-start dormant follicles and improve hair thickness.

Expected Timeline

Week 0: First PRP session

Weeks 4–6: Second session

3–6 months: Noticeable increase in hair density

Individual results vary, but a typical protocol includes 3 treatments spaced 4–6 weeks apart, followed by maintenance sessions every 6–12 months.

Medically Approved Next Steps

PRP is powerful, but it's not the only medically endorsed way to tackle thinning brows. Consider these options, alone or in combination:

1. Topical Treatments

Bimatoprost (Latisse): Originally for eyelash growth, off-label brow use can thicken hairs.

Minoxidil (2% solution): Applied carefully to brows to prolong the growth phase.

Corticosteroid creams: For inflammatory causes (eczema, dermatitis) to reduce follicle damage.

2. Nutritional Optimization

Ensure your diet supports healthy hair growth:

Eat lean protein (eggs, fish, legumes) to supply keratin building blocks.

Supplement iron if you're deficient (confirmed by labs).

Take a daily multivitamin with biotin and zinc.

Stay hydrated—dry skin can impair follicle health.

3. Address Underlying Conditions

Thyroid disorders: Treat hypo- or hyperthyroidism as directed by an endocrinologist.

Alopecia areata: Topical or injected steroids, or immunotherapy under dermatologist supervision.

Skin diseases: Manage seborrheic dermatitis or psoriasis to reduce inflammation around follicles.

4. Cosmetic Camouflage

Microblading or microshading: Semi-permanent pigment to mimic hair strokes.

5. Low-Level Laser Therapy (LLLT)

Safety and Side Effects

PRP and most brow-focused therapies are well-tolerated. Possible side effects include:

Mild pain or bruising at injection sites

Temporary redness or swelling

Rare infection (minimized by sterile technique)

Always choose a board-certified dermatologist or qualified aesthetic provider. Discuss any bleeding disorders or medications that affect clotting before undergoing PRP.

Some eyebrow thinning signs warrant prompt medical attention:

Rapid hair loss over days to weeks

Redness, pain or visible inflammation around brow hairs

Other hair loss in eyebrows, scalp, eyelashes or body

If you experience any of these, please speak to a doctor right away.
