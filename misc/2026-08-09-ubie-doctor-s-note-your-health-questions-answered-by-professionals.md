Diabetic Retinopathy: 4 Stages Your Eye Doctor Checks — and How to Stay in Stage 1

Diabetic retinopathy progresses through four stages, each with increasing risk to your vision: - **Stage 1 (Mild Nonproliferative):** Tiny microaneurysms form in retinal blood vessels, with minimal vision risk. - **Stage 2 (Moderate Nonproliferative):** Blood vessels begin to swell and distort, reducing their ability to transport blood. - **Stage 3 (Severe Nonproliferative):** More blood vessels become blocked, depriving the retina of blood supply and signaling new vessel growth. - **Stage 4 (Proliferative):** The most advanced stage, marked by abnormal new blood vessels, bleeding, retinal detachment, and potential permanent vision loss. **How to stay in Stage 1 and protect your vision:** - Keep blood sugar, blood pressure, and cholesterol within target ranges - Schedule regular dilated eye exams - Maintain healthy habits, including a balanced diet and consistent exercise Because diabetic retinopathy often has no early symptoms, catching subtle changes early is critical to preserving your sight.
