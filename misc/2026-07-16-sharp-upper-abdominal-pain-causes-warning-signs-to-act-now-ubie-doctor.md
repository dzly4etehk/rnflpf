Sharp upper abdominal pain often signals a problem with an organ in the upper belly—such as the stomach, gallbladder, liver, or pancreas. Common causes include inflammation (like gastritis, hepatitis, or pancreatitis), blockages (such as gallstones), infections (like H. pylori or ulcers), and referred pain from nearby organs. The location, timing, and triggers of your pain—along with symptoms like nausea, fever, or jaundice—help narrow down the cause.

Because upper abdominal pain has so many possible causes, ranging from mild to serious, identifying the likely source early is key to getting the right care. Explanation

When you feel a sharp pain in your upper belly, it might be because one of your digestive organs isn't working as it should. For example, an inflamed stomach lining, called gastritis, or an ulcer can cause pain that comes on quickly and feels sharp. Gallstones in your gallbladder might block the bile flow, causing sudden, intense pain in the upper abdomen. Sometimes, the pancreas can become inflamed in a condition called pancreatitis, which also brings sharp pain along with nausea and vomiting. In people with liver problems, the liver might be swollen or irritated and can cause similar discomfort. The pain often tells us that something inside is not working right, and doctors look at your symptoms closely to decide what tests to run. If you have sharp upper abdominal pain, especially if it comes with other symptoms like fever, vomiting, or it does not get better on its own, you can start by using a free symptom checker to help identify possible causes before seeking a proper evaluation from a healthcare professional.
