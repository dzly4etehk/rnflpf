# Why Time's 100 Most Influential List Included Hugh Jackman, John Krasinski, and 7 of Our Other Favorite Men

On Thursday, Time released its list of the 100 most influential people of 2018. From Elon Musk, to Donald Trump, to Rihanna, the list was jam-packed with a diverse array of the world's biggest movers and shakers. To go along with their selections, Time enlisted someone close to each influencer to pen an essay about their contributions to society. So if you've ever wondered what exactly Chris Pratt thinks of the "very strong," "very tall" John Krasinski, Time has you covered. Here's what the magazine had to say about some of our other favorite men who made the list.

Jeff Bezos

Becoming the richest person in the history of the world is a pretty good credential for Time's 100 Most Influential lists. In July, the Amazon chief dethroned Bill Gates to take his place at the top of the list. His estimated worth currently sits just north of $110 billion.

"From the outset, the key to Jeff Bezos’ success has been his intense and unwavering focus on customer satisfaction and on his audience: the end user," writes Jamie Dimon. "He refined and perfected this model in books, and then scaled and expanded it while always keeping consumers front and center, often anticipating their needs before they realized them."

Bulking up at the gym hasn't hurt Bezos, either.

Chadwick Boseman

Prior to February, Chadwick Boseman was an up-and-coming actor with roles as Jackie Robinson and James Brown under his belt. But the 40-year-old became an international superstar after starring as T'Challa in the groundbreaking blockbuster Black Panther, which to date has brought in over $1.3 billion worldwide.

"And now as the Black Panther, he’s inspiring everyone, but especially black youth, who deserve to see superheroes like them, to show them that truly anyone can be a superhero," writes Sean "Diddy" Combs. "This matters, because it has been a long time coming to see our own superheroes and the power that they can have on all of us in society."

Sterling K. Brown

He won an Emmy for portraying Christopher Darden in The People vs. O.J. Simpson, he won an Emmy for his role as Randall in This Is Us, and he hosted Saturday Night Live. Not a bad past few years for the 42-year-old, who also appeared Black Panther as N'Jobu.

"Sterling runs to run; the joy for him is in the doing. That he finds himself vaulted into the firmament—where he belongs—is merely glorious icing," writes fellow People vs. O.J. Simpson Emmy winner Sarah Paulson.

Not mentioned by Time is Brown's on-point ab game.

Kevin Durant

Last June, Kevin Durant proved the haters wrong to take his spot at the top of the NBA when he won Finals MVP. Now he and the rest of the Golden State Warriors are battling through the playoffs to defend their title. He's also getting plenty done off the court.

"From committing $10 million to the College Track program to help disadvantaged kids receive higher education to building and renovating basketball courts around the world so at-risk youth have a safe place to learn and play, KD is giving those growing up in tough situations, like he did, a path to success. His generosity knows no bounds," writes Eddy Cue.

Roger Federer

For a little while there, most people thought the GOAT's days of winning big tennis tournaments was over. Then he won the 2017 Australian Open, his first major since 2012. He followed it up later in the year by winning his eighth Wimbledon, and has already won the Australian Open again in 2018. So yeah, he's not done.

Like Durant, the 36-year-old is also making an impact off the court. "Roger knows that effective philanthropy, like great tennis, requires discipline and time," writes fellow philanthropist Bill Gates. "It will be a sad day for all of us fans when he hangs up his racket—but we can take comfort in knowing that he’s committed to making the world a more equitable place."

Hugh Jackman

Simply calling Hugh Jackman an actor seems inadequate. Yes, he acts, but he also sings, he also dances, he also entertains. His latest stage was the appropriately titled blockbuster The Greatest Showman, which has brought in nearly a half billion dollars at the box office since its release in December. As we've pointed out, he's also knows how to eat a good breakfast, cover up at the beach, and bring it on a stationary bike. Hugh, we salute you.

"I’ve never stopped and analyzed why I love Hugh Jackman," writes Anne Hathaway, Jackman's co-star in Les Miserables. "For me, it’s like loving chocolate or puppies or rainbows: effortless."

Jimmy Kimmel

For most of the past two decades, Jimmy Kimmel was a talk show host. After Donald Trump was elected president, he became something more. The two-time Oscars host has routinely gone after Trump and his cronies while advocating for progressive causes.

"And then Jimmy looked into the camera and told all of us in Washington to get real about health insurance and make sure every baby Billy had a fighting chance. John Krasinski

With The Office now a distant speck in the rearview mirror, Krasinski has blossomed into a true Hollywood powerhouse, writing, directing, and starring in one of 2018's most talked-about films in A Quiet Place. We'll see if a superhero film is in his future.

"My friend John Krasinski is awesome," writes Chris Pratt. "He’s very strong. He’s tall and handsome. He’s married to Emily Blunt. So, you know, there’s that. He is a New England Patriots fan … but nobody’s perfect."

J.J. Watt

J.J. Watt is just about as close as it comes to a real-life superhero. Not only is the 6'5" 290-pound Houston Texans defensive end a physical specimen worthy of rivaling Thor, he raised over $37 million in flood relief after Houston was devastated by Hurricane Harvey last fall.
