Explanation

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy uses your own blood platelets to speed healing and tissue regeneration. While PRP injections or microneedling sessions largely depend on your provider's technique, what you eat in the days leading up to treatment can influence your body's growth factor production and inflammation levels. By focusing on nutrient-dense foods and avoiding inflammatory triggers, you can set the stage for optimal results. Below, we cover evidence-based tips for "PRP and diet for best results," a simple pre-PRP meal plan, and key precautions.

Why Diet Matters for PRP

• Growth factors in platelets help repair tissues, stimulate collagen, and reduce inflammation.• Blood composition reflects recent dietary choices: nutrients you eat today shape your platelet health tomorrow.• A balanced pre-PRP diet can:

Boost platelet count and function

Provide building blocks for collagen and extracellular matrix

Minimize pro-inflammatory compounds that can blunt healing

Key Nutrients to Include

High-Quality Protein

Proteins supply amino acids (especially arginine and lysine) that support platelet synthesis and tissue repair.

Lean poultry, grass-fed beef, wild-caught fish

Eggs

Plant options: lentils, chickpeas, tofu, tempeh

Vitamin C

Acts as a cofactor for collagen formation and supports immune health.

Citrus fruits (oranges, grapefruit)

Berries (strawberries, blueberries)

Red bell peppers, broccoli, kiwi

Zinc

Essential for cell division, immune function, and wound healing.

Shellfish (oysters, crab)

Beef, turkey

Omega-3 Fatty Acids

Anti-inflammatory properties support balanced platelet function.

Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines)

Flaxseeds, chia seeds, walnuts

Collagen or Gelatin (Optional)

Supplemental collagen peptides can provide ready-to-use amino acids for connective tissue.

Mix into smoothies or broths 24–48 hours before treatment.

Hydration and Electrolytes

Adequate fluid intake helps maintain blood volume and concentration of platelets.

Coconut water or electrolyte packets if you sweat heavily

Herbal teas, broths

Foods and Habits to Avoid

To maximize growth factor release and limit interference with platelet function, minimize or skip the following at least 48 hours before PRP:

• Alcohol

Can thin blood and impair platelet aggregation.

• Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs)

Ibuprofen, naproxen, aspirin (unless prescribed). These can reduce platelet activity and blunt the PRP effect.

• Excessive Sugar and Refined Carbs

High blood sugar promotes inflammation, which may counteract the regenerative response.

Lifestyle Tips to Support Platelet Health

Sleep Well: Aim for 7–9 hours of quality sleep. Growth hormone and repair processes peak during deep sleep.

Gentle Movement: Light walking or stretching can support circulation without stressing tissues.

Stress Management: Meditation, deep breathing, or yoga can lower cortisol and inflammatory markers.

Key Takeaways

A nutrient-rich diet 48–72 hours before PRP can support platelet function, boost growth factors, and encourage a balanced inflammatory response.

Focus on lean proteins, vitamin C, zinc, omega-3s, collagen, and proper hydration.

Avoid alcohol, NSAIDs, refined sugars, and highly processed foods.

Maintain good sleep, stress reduction, and moderate activity.

Always discuss serious symptoms or health conditions with your healthcare provider.
