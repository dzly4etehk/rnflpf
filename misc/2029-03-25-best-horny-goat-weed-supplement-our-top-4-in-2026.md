What is horny goat weed?

Horny goat weed is the common name given to plants of the Epimedium genus, a flowering plant native to China and grown throughout that area of the Pacific.8 Some of its other names include barrenwort, bishop’s hat, and fairy wings, but horny goat weed is the one that really stuck.

The legend populating the internet is that a Chinese goat herd noticed increased mating behavior in the members of his flock and connected that behavior to their consumption of a certain local plant. The plant was a weed. The goats were horny. Thus, the name was born.

Whatever the case, Eastern medicine has made use of the plant for centuries, typically as a libido enhancer, but also in cases of Alzheimer’s disease, stroke, and depression.9 Today, libido enhancement remains its most common use, and you’re as likely to see it being sold by a trustworthy company as you are to see it next to the register at a gas station, packaged and sold in a way that would have most reasonable men run screaming in the opposite direction.

But research supports its role in erectile health, at least in animals.3 And further research into its potential benefits to human health has expanded in recent years to include a role in cardiovascular health, neurological wellness, and osteoporosis and other bone conditions.7 10 4

How does horny goat weed work?

Central to any research into horny goat weed’s potential benefits are icariin, a specific flavonoid compounds found in plants of the Epimedium genus that influence various aspects of human health.9 Mechanisms of action are still being investigated, but the results of exposure to icariin in studies reveal real potential in several areas:

Erectile dysfunction (ED)

A significant animal study using 1, 5, and 10mg/kg doses of icariin revealed increases in intracavernous pressure and nitric oxide expressions.11 Interestingly, the 1mg/kg and 10mg/kg doses both outperformed the 5mg/kg dose, though all three doses saw increases compared to placebo. Positive results occurred after four weeks of daily treatment, while an additional group receiving only a single 10mg/kg dose did not outperform the control.3

The implication here is that doses as low as 15mg of icariin may prove beneficial for men weighing around 200lb. However, the largest increase in nitric oxide expression came from the 10mg/kg group, roughly equivalent to 150mg of icariin for that same 200lb man. Unfortunately, high-quality erectile health studies of horny goat weed or icariin in humans have not been published to date.

Cardiovascular disease

In its role in improving erectile health, icariin increases blood flow by releasing nitric oxide and relaxing the muscles that constrict blood vessels.12 That action also reduces blood pressure, which combines with icariin’s apparent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects to reduce the risk of certain cardiovascular diseases, including stroke.7 9 Of course, that drop in blood pressure can be a double-edged sword if you have specific cardiovascular issues or take certain medications that could leave you susceptible to a sudden, dramatic drop in blood pressure. That could cause dizziness, fainting, and death in severe cases.

Osteoporosis and other bone disorders

In a rare human study on icariin, postmenopausal women were given 730mg/day of a horny goat weed extract.4 After six weeks, researchers found a “rise in prenylflavonoid metabolites was associated with higher levels of the bone anabolic marker BSAP, suggesting potential therapeutic value for postmenopausal osteoporosis.”

In experimental models of arthritis, an equivalent of about 1.3g/day of icariin was able to reduce arthritic cartilage degradation and protect against joint loss.13 That’s a significant dose, but it’s below the 1.68g dose at which adverse effects seemed to begin in one safety study.6

Alzheimer’s disease

While human studies are still lacking, animal and in vitro studies show that icariin may improve certain pathologies associated with Alzheimer’s disease.14 This data is extremely recent, and it will require rigorous review and retesting (ideally in humans, eventually) before we can recommend it for Alzheimer’s.

Is horny goat weed safe?

Horny goat weed should be perfectly safe for most people without depression or cardiovascular issues. (Its ability to reduce blood pressure can be a big concern if you’re trying to control your blood pressure for any reason.)7

A large study looking specifically at the safety of icariin (horny goat weed’s primary active component) found doses up to 1,680mg/day to be relatively safe, though some participants in the high-dose group dropped out due to GI discomfort.6 The biggest red flag from that study was a statistically significant increase in self-reported depressive symptoms in the icariin group. This is all the more alarming when you consider that the study only ran for five days.

Since horny goat weed is typically taken daily without any end point in treatment, a five-day study is certainly insufficient to determine long-term safety. Another study using an average dose of horny goat weed extract in postmenopausal women saw no reports of adverse effects over six weeks.4
