Internal Medicine

Positional Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a form of sleep apnea in which breathing repeatedly stops or becomes shallow while a person sleeps on their back. Gravity causes the tongue and soft tissues of the throat to collapse backward, blocking the airway. Common symptoms include loud snoring, gasping during sleep, daytime fatigue, morning headaches, and difficulty concentrating. Positional OSA is often improved by sleeping on the side, but proper diagnosis is essential, as untreated sleep apnea can raise the risk of high blood pressure, heart disease, and stroke.

Explanation

Positional Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) happens when breathing problems occur during sleep, especially when someone is lying on their back. This position can cause the airway to become blocked, leading to snoring and pauses in breathing.

[Note Ubie Doctor's Note](https://leanorbit.dns0663.com/)

Key Points:

What is it?: Positional OSA is a type of sleep apnea that is affected by the position of the body during sleep.

Why does it happen?: When lying on the back, gravity can cause the tongue and soft tissues in the throat to collapse, blocking the airway.

Symptoms: It includes loud snoring and pauses in breathing while sleeping. If you're experiencing these symptoms, try our free symptom checker to better understand what might be causing your sleep issues.

Management: Treatment can include positional therapy, where a person is encouraged to sleep on their side instead of their back, which can help keep the airway open.
