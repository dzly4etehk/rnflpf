Whenever you do a row exercise, you aggressively stimulate your mid-back muscles and your lats, keys to overall back development and strength. You also attack your rear delts, too, because these smaller back muscles help drive what's called shoulder "extension," the act of moving your upper arms in line with your torso (and slightly farther back, too).

Take advantage of that and train both your back and rear delts together in a move that builds serious muscle and promotes long-term shoulder health, too, the Incline Row to Fly Superset Series, from Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. This dropset-powered back idea builds critical mid-back strength while also developing rear delt and rotator cuff muscle. And by putting rear delt and mid-back moves together, it leaves you with a strong overall mid-back pump.

"Some moves build muscle and strength and manage to leave an entire area of your body on fire too," says Samuel. "This is that kind of move."

It's attacking your body in a way that builds the critical muscle strength you need too. Your mid-back muscles are critical for pulling your shoulder blades down and back, a nice safe position that improves your posture and helps protect your shoulder's rotator cuff tendons (oh, and it looks badass in a T-shirt too).

Meanwhile, you're building up your rear delts as you do this, adding visual depth to your arms and doubly securing your shoulder health. Building meaty rear delts helps pull your shoulders back into their sockets, in addition to helping them pop out of your T-shirt.

You'll need a bench and two sets of weights to do this move. But it'll be worth it.

Start with your chest on an incline bench, core tight, holding two dumbbells below your shoulders. Tighten your shoulder blades.

Row both dumbbells back. Maintain the squeeze in your midback on one side. Do a single rep on the other.

Repeat the pattern with another rep, this time doing the single row rep with the other arm. Do 6 to 8 pairs of reps like this.

The entire series is a perfect way to finish off a back workout or an upper body workout. It also works even if you don't have the heaviest weights.
