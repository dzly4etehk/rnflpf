Irregular periods can make it harder to conceive because they often signal inconsistent or absent ovulation. When ovulation doesn't happen on a predictable schedule, timing intercourse to coincide with a fertile window becomes challenging, reducing the likelihood that sperm will meet an egg. Common causes include hormonal imbalances, PCOS, thyroid disorders, and stress, all of which can affect fertility.

If you're experiencing irregular cycles and are concerned about your fertility, understanding the underlying cause is the critical first step. Explanation

When you have irregular periods, the timing of ovulation—when an egg is released—can be unpredictable. Ovulation is a key moment in the menstrual cycle when a woman is most likely to become pregnant. Studies have shown that women with irregular cycles may ovulate less often or at unexpected times. This lowers the chances that sperm will be in the right place at the right time to fertilize an egg.

Irregular cycles can sometimes be a sign of hormonal imbalances or other conditions that affect fertility. In addition, fewer or irregular ovulatory cycles might be linked to higher instances of early embryonal loss, which means that even after conception, the pregnancy might not continue as expected.

If you have irregular periods and are planning a pregnancy, it can be useful to track other signs of ovulation like changes in body temperature or cervical mucus.
